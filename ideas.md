# PC Gaming Luxe — Design Direction

## Three Initial Directions

### Theme Name: Obsidian Atelier
Very Brief Intro: A cinematic editorial world where premium game imagery meets museum-like dark space, brass details, and controlled crimson energy. The mood is rare, collected, and intensely atmospheric.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Chrome Horizon
Very Brief Intro: A polished future-luxury interface built from silver surfaces, midnight navy, cool cyan accents, and crisp modular typography. The mood is precise, fast, and elevated.

### Theme Name: Ember Arcade

## Selected Direction: Obsidian Atelier

### Design Movement
Contemporary luxury editorial design fused with cinematic game key art and a private members-club atmosphere.

### Core Principles
1. Treat every game image as a collectible cover, not a generic thumbnail.
2. Use asymmetry, editorial pacing, and controlled negative space to create authority.
3. Make dark surfaces feel tactile through grain, soft glows, thin metallic rules, and layered depth.
4. Keep interaction restrained and intentional: motion should feel like a gallery reveal, never a noisy arcade effect.

### Color Philosophy
The foundation is near-black obsidian rather than flat black, so imagery can breathe without losing contrast. A signature oxidized-copper accent gives the brand a recognizable luxury signal, while parchment-gold typography creates warmth and scarcity. Crimson is used sparingly as an intensity marker for featured releases and original creations.

### Layout Paradigm
A cinematic, editorial scroll with a wide asymmetrical hero, anchored side labels, horizontal discovery rails, and a dense but breathable catalog. The promotional library should feel like a curated archive; the original games section should read as a private showcase at the end of the journey.

### Signature Elements
1. Vertical copper micro-labels and hairline rules that frame sections like gallery placards.
2. Obsidian glass cards with image-first compositions, subtle grain, and metadata bands.
3. A small copper monogram mark used as a visual seal in navigation, favicon, and original-game badges.

### Interaction Philosophy
Hover states should reveal context instead of merely enlarging cards: reveal genre, year, and a short editorial note through a controlled lift and copper line sweep. Search and filters should feel like archive tools. Download buttons for original games should be clear, grounded, and never disguised as store checkout actions.

### Animation
Use 180–260ms ease-out transitions for controls, 420–700ms staged entrances for hero and section content, and image zoom limited to 1.04–1.06 scale. Stagger card reveals by 45ms. Avoid continuous movement except for a barely visible grain drift and a slow hero image parallax. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Display: Cormorant Garamond, with high-contrast serif forms for titles and the brand lockup. Body and UI: Manrope, used with generous tracking, compact labels, and strong numeric clarity. Headlines should use weight 600–700; metadata is uppercase at 0.16em tracking; body copy is 15–17px with comfortable line height.

### Brand Essence
A private archive of PC game worlds for players who care about atmosphere, craft, and discovery—not just a list of titles. Personality: discerning, cinematic, quietly bold.

### Brand Voice
Headlines sound like editorial invitations, CTAs sound like confident directions, and microcopy stays concise and specific.
Example lines: “Enter the worlds worth remembering.” / “Keep the archive close.”

### Wordmark & Logo
A custom “P” and “G” monogram built as two interlocking angular frames, suggesting a portal and a game viewport. The symbol should be rendered as a bold copper mark without text and used as the navigation seal and favicon.

### Signature Brand Color
Oxidized Copper — #B86B4B. It should appear as a precise signal on obsidian surfaces rather than as a broad decorative wash.
